# Captain James Cook (1728–1779)

## Overview
- **Full name**: Captain James Cook  
- **Born**: 27 October 1728, Marton, England  
- **Died**: 14 February 1779, Kealakekua Bay, Hawaii  
- **Occupation**: British explorer, navigator, cartographer, and Royal Navy captain  
- **Famous for**: His three major voyages that mapped large parts of the Pacific, including Australia, New Zealand, and Hawaii.  

---

## Early Life
- Son of a Scottish farm laborer.  
- Apprenticed in Whitby as a merchant navy seaman.  
- Joined the Royal Navy in 1755 and distinguished himself during the Seven Years’ War.  

---

## Voyages

### First Voyage (1768–1771)
- Ship: *HMS Endeavour*  
- Purpose: Observe the Transit of Venus in Tahiti (1769) and search for the "Terra Australis Incognita."  
- Achievements:  
  - Charted New Zealand’s coastline.  
  - Mapped eastern Australia, claiming it as New South Wales.  

### Second Voyage (1772–1775)
- Ships: *Resolution* and *Adventure*  
- Purpose: To determine the existence of the southern continent.  
- Achievements:  
  - Crossed the Antarctic Circle.  
  - Disproved the existence of a massive southern continent.  
  - Mapped Tonga and Easter Island.  

### Third Voyage (1776–1779)
- Ships: *Resolution* and *Discovery*  
- Purpose: To find the Northwest Passage.  
- Achievements:  
  - Explored the Hawaiian Islands (first European contact).  
  - Charted the Pacific Northwest coast of North America.  
- Death: Killed in Hawaii after a conflict with the islanders.  

---

## Legacy
- Produced some of the most accurate maps of the Pacific of his time.  
- His expeditions greatly expanded European knowledge of the Pacific world.  
- His encounters, however, also marked the beginning of colonial influence and disruption for many Pacific cultures.
